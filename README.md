## How to choose

Start from where your search is stuck, not from the tool with the loudest launch.

| If your problem is... | Look at | Skip for now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Too few interviews from a lot of applications | [Resume tailoring](#resume-builders-and-tailoring), [ATS checkers](#ats-and-keyword-checkers) | More volume. Fix the resume first. |
| Too little time to apply to roles that fit | [Auto-apply agents](#auto-apply-agents) that apply on company sites, or [autofill extensions](#browser-extensions-and-autofill) | Tools that apply to anything with a matching title |
| Not finding roles you want | [Job search and AI job boards](#job-search-and-ai-job-boards) | Auto-apply. It can only apply to what it finds. |
| Interviews that don't convert | [Interview practice](#interview-practice-and-mock-interviews) | Live "copilots" during real interviews |
| An offer in hand | [Salary and negotiation](#salary-and-offer-negotiation) | Anything else. This is the highest-value hour of the search. |
| You code and want full control | [Open source](#open-source-and-self-hosted), [Claude Code setups](#claude-code-and-agent-job-search-setups) | Paid tools that do the same thing |

Four questions sort most tools:

1. **Where does it apply?** On the company's own application form, or by automating LinkedIn? LinkedIn's [User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement) bans bots and its help center lists [prohibited software and extensions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387). Tools that drive your LinkedIn account put it at risk.
2. **Can you see what it sends?** If it writes screening answers you never read, it can say things you can't stand behind in an interview.
3. **Does it filter for fit?** Volume is cheap now. Recruiters notice a candidate who applied to 40 roles at one company.
4. **How does billing work?** Several resume builders sell a cheap trial that renews at a much higher price. Read the renewal line.

## Auto-apply agents

Tools that find roles and submit applications for you. They save the most time and carry the most risk. Read [Using auto-apply without hurting your chances](#using-auto-apply-without-hurting-your-chances) before you turn one on.

Freemium, 10 free applications. (maintainer's tool)

- [AIApply](https://aiapply.co) - Writes a tailored resume, cover letter and follow-up email for each job, with an auto-apply mode on top. Freemium, with a limited free tier.
- [Wobo](https://www.wobo.ai) - Matches you to listings and applies in your voice. Freemium, 5 jobs a day free, Autopilot $44.99 a month.
- [Resumly](https://www.resumly.ai) - Finds jobs, creates a tailored resume for each and applies daily. Freemium, paid from $15 a month billed yearly.
- [JobHire.AI](https://jobhire.ai) - Auto Apply submits to jobs that match your criteria around the clock, alongside a resume builder and tracker. Pricing is not shown on the site.
- [Scale.jobs](https://scale.jobs) - Human assistants apply for you with a custom resume and cover letter per job. Paid, one-time from $399 for 500 applications.
- [ApplyAll](https://www.applyall.com) - People check each job against your criteria and fill the forms, and it skips applications with open questions your resume can't answer. - [Oaki AI](https://www.oaki.io) - Finds jobs on company career sites that fit your resume, tailors the resume and prepares the form answers for each one; a free Chrome extension fills the form for you to submit, and the Roots and Surge plans submit for you within daily limits. Paid, from $10 a week or $50 once, with a trial. Doesn't submit on Workday.

## Using auto-apply without hurting your chances

Auto-apply works when it sends fewer, better applications faster. It hurts when it sends many bad ones.

1. **Fix the resume before you scale.** If ten hand-tailored applications get no replies, a thousand automated ones won't either.
2. **Set hard filters.** Title, seniority, location, visa and salary floor. Anything the tool can't filter on, it will apply to.
3. **Cap volume per company.** One or two roles per company. Recruiters see every application you send them.
4. **Read the answers before they go.** Screening answers about work authorization, salary and years of experience have to be true. Start in a review-first mode and only relax it once the drafts are consistently right.
5. **Prefer company application forms over LinkedIn automation.** Applying on an employer's own form is the same as applying yourself. Tools that log into your LinkedIn account risk a restriction.
6. **Never hand over your LinkedIn password.** A tool that asks for it is the clearest warning sign there is.
7. **Track response rate weekly.** If replies per 100 applications drop, tighten filters instead of adding volume.
8. **Keep applying by hand to the roles you want most.** Use the time saved for referrals and a real note to the hiring manager.
